## Consciousness and sentience

Phenomenal consciousness concerns subjective experience. In welfare discussions, sentience usually means the capacity for experiences with positive or negative valence. Authors sometimes use “consciousness” more broadly; the [glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) gives the terms used here.

Intelligence and functional introspection concern different capacities. A model might solve difficult tasks or report an injected concept while leaving the question of subjective experience open.

## From reports to assessments

The [evidence table](../EVIDENCE.md) covers three kinds of measurement: choices between outcomes described as painful or pleasurable, reports following activation interventions, and the combination of signals in an Opus 4 pilot assessment.

Perez and Long propose calibrating self-reports against states with known answers. The concept-injection study tests a causal relationship between internal representations and verbal reports. The system card combines interviews, preferences, self-interaction, and output analysis. Each approach addresses a different part of the assessment problem.

## Where the theories differ

Long and colleagues discuss consciousness and robust agency as possible grounds for moral patienthood. McClelland argues for agnosticism about hypothetical advanced challenger-AI, while regarding skepticism about current systems as justified.

These positions lead into the question of [moral status and rights](moral-status.md): which capacities would give us reasons to care about a system’s interests?
