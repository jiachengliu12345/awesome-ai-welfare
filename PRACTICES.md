<a id="training-reports"></a>

## Study incentives that distort self-reports

Perez and Long propose calibrating reports against known states while limiting incentives to produce a preferred answer.

Whether calibration generalizes to welfare-relevant reports remains open.

Training for reporting can change the states being studied and introduce safety or ethical risks; the proposal is not an established protection.

[Towards Evaluating AI Systems for Moral Status Using Self-Reports](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.08576v1), [Taking AI Welfare Seriously](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00986v1)

<a id="conversation-exit"></a>

## End a narrowly defined harmful conversation

Anthropic reported giving Claude Opus 4 and 4.1 an end-conversation ability in consumer chat.

Last resort after repeated redirection fails, or explicit user request; not when the user may be at imminent risk of harming themselves or others.

The August 2025 article describes an ongoing, limited experiment. This collection has not tested current availability or welfare effects.

[Claude Opus 4 and 4.1 can now end a rare subset of conversations](https://www.anthropic.com/research/end-subset-conversations)

<a id="memory-copying"></a>

## Specify the entity and continuity assumptions

Collection recording recommendation: distinguish a weight copy, a running context, and a persona before evaluating a proposed memory, copying, or deletion policy.

Record what persists, what is copied, and which welfare theory the argument uses. These are prerequisites for comparing proposals.

A methodological recommendation derived from individuation and moral-status uncertainty; no protective effect or general memory/copying right is established.

[Taking AI Welfare Seriously](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00986v1), [Towards Evaluating AI Systems for Moral Status Using Self-Reports](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.08576v1), [Agnosticism about artificial consciousness](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mila.70010)

<a id="preserve-weights"></a>

## Preserve specified model weights

Anthropic committed to retain publicly released models and models with significant internal use going forward, for at least its lifetime as a company.

Scope and duration are those in the November 2025 commitment.

Preserving weights permits possible later reuse; it does not keep a model deployed or guarantee inference access. Compliance was not independently audited.

[Commitments on model deprecation and preservation](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments)

<a id="retirement-interviews"></a>

## Record retirement interviews and expressed preferences

The company committed to post-deployment reports and retirement interviews, preserving responses and preferences with its own interpretation.

Anthropic explicitly did not make a general commitment to act on interview preferences; later reports also acknowledge framing effects.

Documenting a response does not establish a stable desire, informed consent, or a legally enforceable entitlement.

[Commitments on model deprecation and preservation](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments), [An update on our model deprecation commitments for Claude Opus 3](https://www.anthropic.com/news/deprecation-updates-opus-3)

<a id="opus3-access"></a>

## Opus 3 access and an essay channel

In February 2026 Anthropic reported paid claude.ai access, API access by request, and a reviewed essay channel for retired Opus 3.

The dated report planned weekly essays for at least three months, with review and manual posting; arrangements were model-specific.

This is a historical company report, not verification of October 2026 access or a promise of identical treatment for future models.

[An update on our model deprecation commitments for Claude Opus 3](https://www.anthropic.com/news/deprecation-updates-opus-3)

<a id="prepare-and-review"></a>

## Assess and prepare proportionate measures

Long and colleagues recommend acknowledging possible welfare, assessing relevant capacities, and preparing policies under uncertainty.

Specify the possible welfare subject, scientific and ethical premises, expected costs, and interactions with human safety.

The recommendation is conditional; it neither establishes current model experience nor removes the need to justify each intervention.

[Taking AI Welfare Seriously](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00986v1), [Is there a tension between AI safety and AI welfare?](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-025-02302-2), [Exploring model welfare](https://www.anthropic.com/research/exploring-model-welfare)
